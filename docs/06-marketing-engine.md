# JC Coaching — the marketing engine

The operating manual. `docs/05-content-engine.md` says how one blog post becomes
twelve social pieces. This document is the machine around it: every asset John
owns, the funnel those assets feed, the production line that makes them, the
exact specifications for each file, and when to turn on paid advertising.

Read `docs/03-business-strategy.md` for channels and `docs/04-growth-plan.md`
for the capacity model those channels are feeding.

---

## 0. The short version

1. **He has more assets than most six-figure practices.** Three published
   journals, 65 articles, 43 quotable lines in his own words, nine
   testimonials, an ICF credential, 12 years of HR, and a personal story. The
   problem has never been raw material.
2. **The journals are the missing funnel layer.** A $10 book on Amazon is a
   lead magnet a stranger pays for. It also gives him a keynote, a cohort
   workbook, and an "author of three books" line that changes how every other
   asset reads.
3. **Four funnel doors, one per temperature.** A stranger gets a video. A
   reader gets the burnout check. A subscriber gets a book or a call. A caller
   gets a package. Nothing skips a step.
4. **One filming day a month makes twelve videos for three platforms.** The
   specifications in §4 are the whole production standard. Follow them and
   every file exports once and posts everywhere.
5. **Templates, not designs.** Five Canva templates built once in the site's
   real colours. After that, making a carousel is typing, not designing.
6. **Advertising comes last and starts at $15 a day.** Organic first, retarget
   second, cold traffic only once a landing page has converted warm traffic.
7. **Everything is tagged.** A booked call traces back to the platform, the
   post and the button. That is what makes month four a decision instead of a
   guess.

### 1.1 The journals are the underused asset

Three published books is a credential most coaches never get. Right now they sit
on a page that is not in the navigation. What they unlock:

- **"Author of three journals" in every bio.** It is the single cheapest
  credibility upgrade available and it costs nothing to deploy.
- **A paid lead magnet.** Someone who spends $12 on his book is a warmer lead
  than someone who downloads a free PDF. Amazon does the fulfilment. Put a
  "start with the journal" path on the site for people not ready for $1,000.
- **The cohort workbook.** The six-week group programme in
  `docs/04-growth-plan.md` §6 ships with the Rest Regimen journal. Product cost
  ten dollars, perceived value far higher.
- **The client gift.** Every new partnership client gets one in the mail.
- **The keynote.** "The Highlight of My Day" is a talk, a practice, and a book
  with the same name. That is what a speaking pitch looks like.
- **Ad creative.** A physical book in hand is the best-performing image type
  for an author. It reads as legitimacy in a feed full of coaches.
- **Reviews matter.** Amazon reviews are social proof he can ask every happy
  client for, and they cost the client two minutes.

One caution: do not build the marketing around selling books. The books are a
trust device and a funnel entrance. The revenue is coaching.

## 2. The funnel

Four doors, one per temperature. Each stage has one job: move the person one
step, not five.

```
  COLD                  WARM                   HOT                  CLIENT
  ────                  ────                   ───                  ──────
  Short video     →     Blog post        →     Intro call     →     Package
  Quote card            Burnout check          Single session       Continuation
  Carousel              Newsletter             Journal purchase     Referral
  Search result         LinkedIn follow        Workshop attendee    Testimonial

  Platform              The site               Calendly             The review
                                                                    session
```

### 2.1 Stage by stage

| Stage | Who they are | The one job | The asset | The measure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold** | Never heard of John | Stop the scroll, earn one click | Short video, quote card, carousel, a search result | Click-throughs to the site |
| **Warm** | On the site, reading | Get an email address | Blog post, burnout check, newsletter signup | Check completions |
| **Nurture** | On the list | Build trust until they are ready | Newsletter every two weeks, check sequence, the journals | Opens, replies, book sales |
| **Hot** | Considering | Book the free call | Coaching page, FAQ, testimonials, pricing | Intro calls booked |
| **Client** | Paying | Deliver, then extend | The engagement, the review session | Tenure, referrals |

### 2.2 The org funnel runs in parallel

Different doors, same engine.

```
  LinkedIn comment on an HR leader's post
        ↓
  Their profile visit → his profile, headline, featured section
        ↓
  A workshop proof post or an org testimonial
        ↓
  Coffee / a call
        ↓
  One-pager + starting prices
        ↓
  Lunch-and-learn
        ↓
  Workshop series → retainer → individual clients from the room
```

The asset gap here is one PDF: a capabilities one-pager with the seven topics,
formats, group sizes, three org testimonials, credentials and a starting price.
It is on the launch checklist and it is the highest-value single document in
the entire marketing system, because one workshop equals ten client-months.

### 2.3 What each entrance links to

| Entrance | Links to | Why not somewhere else |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram bio | `/burnout-check` | Cold traffic will not buy $1,000. It will spend two minutes. |
| TikTok bio | `/burnout-check` | Same. |
| YouTube description | The blog post, then the check | Search traffic wants the full answer first. |
| LinkedIn featured | The check, one workshop testimonial, `/for-organizations` | Both buyers land here. Give them both a door. |
| LinkedIn post | The post, in the first comment | A link in the body suppresses reach. |
| Newsletter | One post per issue | One link per email converts better than three. |
| Amazon book | The site, in the back matter | The only place a reader goes looking. |
| Google Business Profile | `/coaching` | Local search is high intent. Send it to the money page. |

## 3. The production line

Three sittings a month, about six hours total, on the protected day from
`docs/04-growth-plan.md` §5.

### 3.1 Sitting one: pick and write (2 hours)

1. Open `docs/content-bank.md`. Pick eight posts for the month from the season
   table in `docs/05-content-engine.md` §7.
2. For each post, the bank already gives you the hook, the subheads, the
   numbered list, the quotable lines and the closing. Paste that entry into a
   working doc.
3. Write, in this order, because each one shortens the next:
   - The LinkedIn post (the longest form).
   - The video script (cut from the LinkedIn post).
   - The carousel slides (the numbered list, one per slide).
   - The captions (the LinkedIn post, halved).
   - The X posts (the quotable lines, as they are).
4. A drafting assistant is fine for the first cut. Give it the bank entry plus
   `docs/brand/voice.md` and ask for the five formats. John edits every one.
   Nothing publishes in a voice that is not his.

Output: one working doc with 8 LinkedIn posts, 12 video scripts, 4 carousels,
8 quote card lines, 20 X posts.

### 3.2 Sitting two: film (2 hours)

Twelve videos in one session. The specification is in §4.1. Change shirts every
four videos so a month of posts does not look like one afternoon. Film all
twelve before editing any.

### 3.3 Sitting three: build and schedule (2 hours)

1. Captions and cuts in CapCut. Export once per video at the §4.1 spec.
2. Carousels and quote cards from the Canva templates in §4.3. Typing, not
   designing.
3. Load everything into the scheduler with dates and the UTM links from §6.
4. Publish the month. Then leave it alone.

## 4. Specifications

This section exists so nobody has to make these decisions twice.

### 4.1 Vertical video (TikTok, Reels, Shorts)

One file, three platforms.

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Aspect and size | 9:16, 1080 × 1920 |
| Frame rate | 30 fps |
| Length | 30 to 60 seconds. Under 45 performs best cold. |
| Audio | Clip-on lavalier microphone, about $25. This matters more than the camera. |
| Camera | Phone rear camera, on a tripod, at eye level. Not held. |
| Light | Face a window. Never a window behind him. No overhead light alone. |
| Framing | Eyes on the upper third line. Head and shoulders. A little headroom. |
| Background | One consistent corner. Plain wall, a shelf, or his office. The three journals visible on the shelf is not an accident. |
| Captions | Burned in, always. Most people watch muted. |
| Safe zones | Keep text out of the top 250 px and the bottom 420 px. Platform interface covers both. |
| Hook | First two seconds, said out loud and on screen. A line from the bank. |
| End | "The full piece is on the blog." No hard sell in a cold video. |

**The five-beat script**, same every time:

```
0:00  Hook. A quotable line. No name, no intro, no "hey guys".
0:03  The problem. "Most people I work with…"
0:12  The point. One idea only. Usually a subhead from the post.
0:30  One thing to try this week.
0:45  Where to go. Blog for cold, free call for warm.
```

**Batch discipline:** film all twelve, then edit all twelve, then caption all
twelve. Switching tasks per video triples the time.

### 4.2 Thumbnails and covers

| Platform | What it needs | Spec |
|---|---|---|
| **TikTok / Reels** | A cover frame, chosen not defaulted | Pick a frame where his eyes are open and he is mid-expression. Add the hook line as three to five words, large. |
| **YouTube Shorts** | Same file, but the title is the search asset | Title with the keyword: "Signs of burnout at work". The thumbnail matters less on Shorts; the first frame matters more. |
| **YouTube long-form** (later) | A real thumbnail | 1280 × 720. His face on one side, three to five words on the other, in the site green `#365347` with white type. Readable at the size of a postage stamp. |
| **Blog post / link preview** | Open Graph image | 1200 × 630. Post title, John's name small, site colours. This is what LinkedIn and iMessage show. Sixty-five of these are needed; the template makes it a batch job. |
| **Podcast / talk clips** | 1:1 or 4:5 versions | Re-export the vertical file into a square with the caption bar top and bottom. |

**Thumbnail rules that hold everywhere:** at most five words, one idea, a real
face, high contrast, never a stock photo, never the same words as the title
underneath it.

### 4.3 The five Canva templates

Built once, in the site's actual colours so social and site read as one brand.
From `src/styles/tokens.css`:

| Role | Value |
|---|---|
| Deep green (the brand colour, backgrounds and CTAs) | `#365347` |
| White (text on green, page background) | `#ffffff` |
| Ink (body text on white) | `#121212` |
| Grey (alternate bands, cards) | `#f1f1f1` |
| Muted (captions, attribution) | `#6e6e6e` |
| Yellow (one accent mark only, never a background) | `#ffcb05` |

Type: Bitter for headings, matching the site. A clean sans for body.

| # | Template | Size | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Quote card** | 1080 × 1080 | Green background. One line from the bank in white Bitter, large, centred. "John Chambers · ICF ACC" small at the bottom. No photo. |
| 2 | **Carousel** | 1080 × 1350 (4:5) | Eight slides. Slide 1 title on green. Slides 2 to 6 white with a green headline. Slide 7 "If this is you." Slide 8 the CTA on green. |
| 3 | **Link preview** | 1200 × 630 | White, post title in ink, a green rule, his name and credential small. Doubles as the site's Open Graph image. |
| 4 | **Video cover** | 1080 × 1920 | A transparent text overlay: hook line, three to five words, white on a green bar. Dropped over a chosen frame. |
| 5 | **Org one-pager** | US Letter PDF | Topics, formats, group sizes, three testimonials, credentials, starting price, contact. The document from §2.2. |

**How a carousel actually gets made,** start to finish, about eight minutes
once the template exists:

1. Open the bank entry for a list post. It has the numbered items already.
2. Duplicate template 2. Slide 1: paste the post title.
3. Slides 2 to 6: paste one bolded item lead per slide as the headline, one
   sentence under it.
4. Slide 7: the closing line from the bank.
5. Slide 8: one CTA. The burnout check for Instagram, the intro call for
   LinkedIn. Never both.
6. Export twice. PNG set for Instagram. A single PDF for LinkedIn, which is
   what makes it a document post.

**How a LinkedIn post gets made:** it is written in sitting one and pasted into
the scheduler in sitting three. The only per-post work is the first comment,
which carries the link. Schedule the post, then add the comment manually at
publish time or let the scheduler do it if it supports first comments.

**How an Instagram post gets made:** the carousel PNGs or the video file, the
halved caption, and the bio link never changes. Stories reshare the same asset
the day it goes up.

## 5. The advertising engine

Organic first. Ads amplify a thing that already works; they do not create one.
Three gates, in order.

### 5.1 Gate one: retargeting only (month 4 onward)

Prerequisite: GA4 and the pixels are live, and the site has had traffic for at
least a month.

- **Who:** people who visited the site or watched 50% of a video, in the last
  30 days.
- **Budget:** $5 to $10 a day. This is a tiny audience and it does not need
  more.
- **Creative:** a testimonial quote card and a 30-second video of John saying
  what the intro call actually is.
- **Destination:** `/burnout-check` for video viewers, `/coaching` for site
  visitors.
- **Why first:** retargeting is the cheapest conversion in advertising because
  the audience is already warm. If this does not work, cold traffic definitely
  will not.

### 5.2 Gate two: Google Search, local (month 4 onward, can run alongside)

- **Budget:** $10 to $15 a day.
- **Terms:** "burnout coach Austin", "executive coach Austin", "career coach
  Austin", "burnout coaching". Exact and phrase match. Negative keywords for
  "free", "certification", "jobs", "salary".
- **Destination:** `/coaching`, not the homepage.
- **Why:** highest intent traffic that exists. Someone typing this has already
  decided they need a coach. Low competition locally.
- **Read it at 30 days:** cost per intro call, not cost per click. If an intro
  call costs less than $150 and one in three becomes a client, that is a
  $1,000 package for $450 of ads. Scale it. If it costs more, pause and go back
  to organic.

### 5.3 Gate three: cold social, only if gates one and two pay (month 7+)

- **Meta (Instagram and Facebook), $20 to $30 a day.** Creative is the three
  best-performing organic videos, unchanged. Never make an ad; promote what
  already worked.
- **Destination:** `/burnout-check`. Never the coaching page. Cold social
  traffic converts on an email address, not on $1,000.
- **Then retarget the check-takers** with the intro call over the next 14 days.
- **LinkedIn ads: no.** Minimum spend makes sense for $20,000 deals, not
  $2,000 ones. His LinkedIn strategy is organic plus comments, permanently.

### 5.4 What gets advertised, in priority order

1. The burnout check. Always. It is the cheapest conversion and it fills the
   list that everything else sells to.
2. A journal. Low-friction, builds authority, and Amazon handles delivery.
3. The intro call, to warm audiences only.
4. A workshop or the org one-pager, to a job-title audience, only if he wants
   more org work than his network produces.

### 5.5 The numbers that decide

Run every campaign against one number: **cost per intro call held.**

| Result | Action |
|---|---|
| Under $150 | Scale the budget 20% a week |
| $150 to $300 | Keep, optimise creative |
| Over $300 for 30 days | Pause. The problem is upstream, not the budget. |

Never judge an ad by impressions, reach, cost per click or followers.

## 6. Tracking, end to end

Every link carries its origin so a booked call can be traced back to the piece
that made it.

- **Social links:**
  `?utm_source=<platform>&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=<post-slug>`
- **Ads:** `utm_medium=paid` and the campaign name.
- **Newsletter:** `utm_medium=email`.
- **Site CTAs:** already handled by `ctaHref()` in `src/config/cta.ts`, which
  tags every button with its page and position.
- **Events:** `src/lib/analytics.ts` already defines `cta_click`,
  `lead_magnet_submit` and `calendly_booked`. Wiring GA4 is pasting the tag and
  adding one listener, not designing a measurement plan.
- **Calendly passes UTMs through to the booked event**, which means the source
  of a client is visible on the calendar invitation.

**The monthly review**, first of the month, fifteen minutes:

| Question | Where to look |
|---|---|
| How many intro calls, and from where? | Calendly UTMs |
| How many check completions, and from where? | Kit, tagged by source |
| Which posts sent traffic? | GA4 landing pages |
| Which platform sent nothing? | GA4 source report |
| What did an intro call cost, if ads are running? | Ad spend ÷ calls held |

Then do the obvious thing: more of what worked, drop what sent nothing after
three months.
