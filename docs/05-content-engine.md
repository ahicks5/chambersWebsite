# JC Coaching — content engine

How the 65 blog posts become LinkedIn posts, Instagram carousels and reels,
TikToks, YouTube Shorts, X posts and newsletter issues, without John writing
anything new for six months.

Companion to `docs/03-business-strategy.md` (channels) and
`docs/04-growth-plan.md` (the one protected day a week this runs on). The raw
material is inventoried in `docs/content-bank.md`, generated by
`scripts/content-bank.mjs`.

`docs/06-marketing-engine.md` is the machine around this one: the full asset
inventory including the three journals, the funnel those pieces feed, file
specifications for video and graphics, and the advertising gates.

---

## 0. The short version

1. **The blog is the canon.** Every social piece is a cut of a post, and every
   cut links back to that post. The post links to the burnout check and the
   intro call. Social exists to send people to the site, not to be the site.
2. **One post yields about twelve pieces.** A LinkedIn post, a carousel, two
   or three short videos posted to three platforms from one file, two quote
   cards, a handful of X posts, and a newsletter issue.
3. **Cut 24 posts, not 65.** A twelve-week season at two posts a week. The
   bank says which 24. The other 41 are seasons two and three.
4. **One video file, three platforms.** TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube
   Shorts take the same vertical file. Record twelve in one sitting a month.
5. **LinkedIn is where he talks to people. Everywhere else is broadcast.** Reply
   to comments on LinkedIn and Instagram. Schedule the rest and leave it.
6. **Only John's lines on quote cards.** The bank has 43 of them. No stock
   quotes, no HBR statistics as graphics.
7. **Measure clicks to the site, not followers.** Every link carries a source
   tag. The monthly question is which platform booked intro calls.

## 2. The recipe: one post, twelve pieces

The shape of the post decides the cuts. The bank labels every post.

### 2.1 A list post (24 of them)

Example: "Reclaim Your Focus: Strategies to Combat Meeting Fatigue" (four
numbered strategies, three research bullets, one quotable line).

| Piece | Platform | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Carousel | Instagram, LinkedIn (as a PDF document post) | Slide 1: the title. One slide per numbered item, bolded lead as the slide headline, one sentence under it. Second-to-last slide: "If this is you." Last slide: CTA. |
| Thread | X, Threads | The title, then one post per numbered item, then the link. |
| Short videos, one per item | TikTok, Reels, Shorts | 30 to 45 seconds each. "Here is the one thing that beats meeting fatigue that nobody does." |
| One "all of them" video | TikTok, Reels, Shorts | 60 seconds, text on screen for each item. |
| LinkedIn text post | LinkedIn | The hook paragraph, the list with one line each, one question at the end. |
| Quote card | Instagram feed, LinkedIn image | "Don't shrink to survive, expand to lead." |
| Newsletter issue | Kit | Two sentences and the link. |

### 2.2 A story post (11 of them)

Example: "Burnout Is a Liar" (a message from a stranger, a turn, a practice,
three quotable lines).

| Piece | Platform | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Talking-head video | TikTok, Reels, Shorts | John tells the opening scene to camera in 45 to 60 seconds. Hook is the first quotable line. Ends on the practice. |
| LinkedIn story post | LinkedIn | First paragraph as the hook. The turn. The lesson. One line about the practice. A question. |
| Two quote cards | Instagram, LinkedIn | "Burnout doesn't reveal who we really are. It distorts it." and "That is not the truth of who you are." |
| Three X posts | X, Threads | Each quotable line on its own, one a day. |
| Practice video | TikTok, Reels, Shorts | 30 seconds on the one practice in the post (text yourself every win). |
| Newsletter issue | Kit | The scene, the line, the link. |

### 2.3 A thesis post (30 of them)

Example: "The Definition of Burnout Is Broken" (an argument, a named
research source, a reframe).

| Piece | Platform | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Hot-take post | LinkedIn | The title as the first line. Three short paragraphs: what everyone believes, why it is wrong, what to believe instead. A question. |
| Explainer video | TikTok, Reels, Shorts | 60 seconds. "The official definition of burnout is failing us. Here is why." |
| Myth-versus-truth carousel | Instagram, LinkedIn | Slide per myth, slide per correction. |
| Quote cards | Instagram, LinkedIn | The bolded lines. |
| X posts | X, Threads | The title plus one line, then two of the quotable lines. |
| Newsletter issue | Kit | The argument in three sentences and the link. |

### 2.4 What a piece never does

- Never introduces a new CTA label. It is "the full piece is on the blog" or
  "Book a free intro call", never "DM me" or "link in bio to work with me".
- Never quotes a client in a way they could be recognized. Details change,
  the point stays.
- Never uses the cut list words in `docs/brand/voice.md`. The blog avoids them
  and social should too.
- Never posts the same caption on every platform. Same idea, native shape.

## 3. Platform playbooks

### 3.1 LinkedIn (primary)

Both buyers are here. This is the only platform that gets John's replies.

- **Cadence:** three posts a week. Monday a hot take (thesis), Wednesday a
  carousel (list), Friday a story.
- **Format:** the first line is the hook and it must work before "see more":
  under twelve words, no emoji, no "I'm excited to share". Short paragraphs.
  One question at the end, because comments are what LinkedIn rewards.
- **Links:** in the first comment, or "the full piece is on the blog". A link
  in the body reaches fewer people.
- **Document posts** (a PDF carousel) reach more people than image posts. Every
  list post becomes one.
- **Rooted Reflections as a LinkedIn Newsletter,** monthly, the pillar piece
  of that month. Launching one notifies every connection.
- **Ten minutes a day** commenting on posts by HR leaders and people leaders.
  That is where org conversations start. Not on his own posts.
- **Profile:** headline is the positioning line, not "Coach & Consultant".
  Featured section: the burnout check, one workshop testimonial, one post.

### 3.2 Instagram

Colder audience, more individuals, fewer buyers. It is where the burnout check
does its work.

- **Cadence:** three reels a week (the same files as TikTok and Shorts), one
  carousel a week, one quote card a week. Stories: repost each piece the day
  it goes up, nothing more.
- **Bio link:** the burnout check, not the homepage. Instagram traffic is not
  ready for a $1,000 decision. It is ready for two minutes and an email.
- **No links in captions.** They do not work. "Link in bio" and the check.
- **Captions:** the LinkedIn post, shortened by half, with the question kept.

### 3.3 TikTok, Reels and YouTube Shorts (one file)

One vertical video, 9:16, 30 to 60 seconds, captions burned in, posted to all
three. Three a week.

- **The hook is a quotable line from the bank, said in the first two
  seconds.** "Burnout doesn't reveal who you are. It distorts you." Then the
  point. Then one thing to try. Then "the full piece is on the blog."
- **Record twelve in one sitting** once a month, on the protected day. Phone
  on a stand, window light, a $20 clip-on microphone, change shirts every
  four videos so a month of posts does not look like one afternoon.
- **YouTube Shorts is search.** Title each Short with the keyword: "Signs of
  burnout at work", "How to rest without guilt". Shorts surface in Google
  years later. TikTok and Reels do not.
- **TikTok is where he already has a habit and strangers already message
  him.** Keep it. Reply to messages once a week, not daily.
- **Later, not now:** one long YouTube video a month (eight to ten minutes,
  the pillar post read and expanded to camera). Its audio is a podcast episode
  for free. Start after the Shorts are steady.

### 3.4 X and Threads

Lowest priority. Schedule and leave.

- One post a day: a quotable line, or one item from a list, or a title plus
  a line. Threads for the list posts.
- No engagement effort. If it produces nothing in the monthly numbers after
  three months, stop and lose nothing.

### 3.5 Newsletter (Kit)

- Every two weeks. One post, two sentences, one link. The subject line is the
  post title.
- The burnout check sequence (three emails: the result, the practice, the
  intro call) runs automatically underneath.
- Once a quarter: the alumni "how is it holding" email from
  `docs/04-growth-plan.md` §4.5, to past clients only.

## 4. Production: the monthly batch

This runs on the one protected day a week from `docs/04-growth-plan.md` §5.
About six hours a month, in three sittings.

| Sitting | Time | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Pick and cut** | 2 hours | Choose the month's eight posts from the bank. For each, paste the bank entry into a doc and write the LinkedIn post, the carousel slides and the video scripts. The bank already has the hook, the subheads, the list and the quotes, so this is editing, not writing. |
| **Record** | 2 hours | Twelve videos from the scripts. Captions in CapCut. Export once. |
| **Design and schedule** | 2 hours | Carousels and quote cards from three Canva templates. Load everything into a scheduler for the month. |

Tools, all cheap or free:

- **Canva.** Three templates, built once, in the site's colours from
  `src/styles/tokens.css` so social and site look like one thing: a quote
  card (1080 square), a carousel (1080 square, eight slides), and a 1200×630
  link image. The link image doubles as the post's Open Graph image, which is
  on the launch checklist anyway.
- **CapCut** (free) for captions and cuts.
- **Metricool** or **Buffer** to schedule LinkedIn, Instagram, TikTok,
  YouTube and X from one place. Both have free tiers that cover this volume.
- **Kit** for the newsletter, per `docs/03-business-strategy.md` §5.4.
- **A drafting assistant is fine for first cuts.** Each bank entry plus
  `docs/brand/voice.md` is enough context to draft twelve pieces. John edits
  every one; nothing goes out in a voice that is not his.

## 5. Templates

### 5.1 LinkedIn post

```
[Hook: the quotable line or the title, under twelve words]

[Two or three lines: the scene or the problem, in second person where it fits]

[The turn: what is actually going on]

[One practice, concrete]

[One question]

The full piece is on the blog. Link in the comments.
```

### 5.2 Short video script (30 to 60 seconds)

```
0:00  Hook, to camera, the quotable line. No intro, no name.
0:03  The problem in one sentence. "Most people I work with…"
0:12  The point. One idea. The subhead that carries it.
0:30  One thing to try this week.
0:45  "The full piece is on the blog. And if this is where you are,
       there is a free intro call. No pitch."
```

Text on screen: the hook line, then the practice. Nothing else.

### 5.3 Carousel (eight slides)

```
1  Title, as written. John's photo small in the corner.
2  The problem in one line.
3-6  One item per slide. Bolded lead as the headline. One sentence under it.
7  "If this is you." Two lines from the closing paragraph.
8  "Take the 2-minute burnout check" or "Book a free intro call".
   One CTA per carousel, not both.
```

### 5.4 Quote card

One line from the bank. John's words only. His name and "ICF ACC" small at the
bottom. Site colours. No photo, no stock texture.

## 6. Worked example: "Burnout Is a Liar"

The bank entry gives: a hook (a message from a stranger on TikTok), three
subheads, three quotable lines, a practice (a running record of wins), and a
closing line. Twelve pieces:

**LinkedIn (Friday story)**

> Burnout doesn't reveal who you are. It distorts you.
>
> A stranger messaged me this week. She is so burned out in her current job
> that she no longer feels good enough to work anywhere else.
>
> That is not a performance problem. It is not a confidence problem. It is
> what chronic stress does to the part of you that believes you can handle
> things.
>
> One of my clients started texting herself every time she got a compliment
> or a win. A record she can read on the days burnout tells her a different
> story.
>
> What would be in your record from this week?
>
> The full piece is on the blog. Link in the comments.

**Video 1 (talking head, 50 seconds).** Hook: "Burnout doesn't reveal who you
are. It distorts you." The message from the stranger. The line about
self-efficacy. "Start a note on your phone. Every win goes in it. Read it on
the bad days." Close: the blog and the free call.

**Video 2 (practice, 30 seconds).** "The simplest thing I did in my own worst
burnout." Three highlights a night before bed. Two weeks to notice.

**Carousel (Instagram and LinkedIn document).** Slides: title · "She no longer
feels good enough to work anywhere else" · What burnout does to identity ·
It is a symptom, not a verdict · Build the record · Three ways to start (a
note, an email thread, a friend) · If this is you · Take the 2-minute burnout
check.

**Quote cards (two).** "Burnout doesn't reveal who we really are. It distorts
it." and "That is not the truth of who you are."

**X (three, one a day).** Each quotable line on its own. Day three adds the
link.

**Newsletter.** Subject: "Burnout is a liar". Two sentences, the link.

That is twelve pieces from 700 words, and none of it is new writing.

## 8. Links and measurement

- **Every social link points at the post,** tagged
  `utm_source=<platform>&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=<post-slug>`. The
  post's author box and end-of-post CTA (already planned, audit §7.4) carry
  the page-level tags from `src/config/cta.ts`, so a booked call can be traced
  from the platform to the post to the button.
- **Instagram bio** links to `/burnout-check?utm_source=instagram`.
- **Monthly, on the first,** alongside the numbers in
  `docs/04-growth-plan.md` §7: site sessions by platform, burnout-check
  signups by platform, intro calls by platform (Calendly shows the UTMs).
  Followers are not on the list.
- **After three months,** drop whichever platform sent nothing. Keep whichever
  sent calls, even if the follower count is small.

## 10. Where the site helps

Three items already on the launch checklist matter more once this runs:

- **Post hero images.** Each post needs a 1200×630 image for a link to look
  right on LinkedIn. The Canva link template produces them. One pass, 65
  images, done.
- **The author box and end-of-post CTA on every post** (audit §7.4). Social
  sends people to the post; the post has to send them somewhere.
- **The burnout check as a page, not a PDF** (`docs/03-business-strategy.md`
  §5.4). Instagram and TikTok traffic land there, and a page that gives a
  result on the spot converts cold traffic far better than a download.

Re-run `node scripts/content-bank.mjs` after any change to the posts. It is
read-only against the content and takes a second.
